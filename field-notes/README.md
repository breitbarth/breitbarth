# Field Notes
*Lessons from the field, not just the dashboard.*

Aaron Breitbarth's practical sales leadership methods, reconstructed and generalized for public learning. No employer-specific confidential information, customer data or personal personnel records. Illustrations below are fictional or generalized; no measured improvement is claimed.

| Method | Problem | Example | Confirmation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Territory activation | Sales assignments lag availability | Coordinate survey, construction and sales to confirm serviceable locations and assign eligible areas when ready | Time from confirmed availability to assignment; reconcile eligibility |
| Coaching loops | Activity alone misses execution gaps | Observe a representative's opening, agree one behavior change, then revisit it | Observed behavior and relevant conversion denominator |
| Training with buy-in | Training becomes passive attendance | Short scenarios, practice, peer feedback and one field application | Demonstrated skill and subsequent usage |
| Incentives and recognition | Identical incentives do not motivate everyone equally | Ask preference; offer fair, policy-compliant recognition for defined achievements | Participation and retention; check fairness and adverse effects |
| Recruiting and onboarding | Hiring decisions become inconsistent | Define role competencies, structured interview, practice milestones and review | Consistent scoring, documented training outcomes |
| Performance feedback | Unclear expectations erode trust | State observable gap, expected outcome, support, date and review | Recorded progress against agreed threshold |

## Working principle
Process, problem, example, impact, confirm and relevant features. Distinguish observed management experience from generalized proposals and future experiments.
