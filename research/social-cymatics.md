# Social Cymatics

Author and originator: Aaron Anthony Breitbarth. AI-assisted research note. Published November 20, 2026.

## Process
Study reciprocal feedback between individual interactions and the institutional structures that organize them.

## Problem
Models that treat institutional rules as fixed may overlook the cumulative effects of behavior on those rules.

## Example
A vibrating plate organizes sand; repeated contact might also alter the plate. This is an analogy, not evidence of a social mechanism or a claim about any particular physical apparatus.

## Proposed mechanism
Let institutional state S and aggregate behavior B evolve jointly: S(t+1)=F(S(t),B(t)); B(t+1)=G(B(t),S(t)). Reinforcement, erosion and thresholds are candidate dynamics, not universal laws.

## Impact
Potential applications include organizational change, institutional resilience and path dependence.

## Confirm
Compare agent-based simulations with fixed versus adaptive institutional rules under matched starting conditions. Measure persistence, response to shocks and threshold crossings. Test sensitivity to update rules and compare against null models. No simulation results or empirical validation yet.

## Relevant features
Reciprocal causation, structural memory, cumulative interaction and feedback. Literature review leads: complex adaptive systems, institutional economics, coevolution and path dependence.
