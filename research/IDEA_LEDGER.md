# Research idea ledger

Maintained for Aaron Anthony Breitbarth. This is a durable record of recovered discussion themes, examples and proposed investigations, not a transcript or proof of novelty. Dates identify recovered discussions, not independently verified priority. AI-assisted synthesis; preserve original author contributions and distinguish later suggestions.

## Thought Math (September 4 and October 2, 2026)
Question: which relationships survive translation across sensory representations, and which become easier or harder to notice? Example: 3 × 4 as notation, three groups of four, rhythm, tactile objects and narrative. Test independent novel-problem transfer, hidden relationship discovery or improved questions, rather than merely correct repetition or preference. AI might discover valid mathematics without supplying human-usable conceptual handles. See [research framework](../thought-math/README.md) and [example laboratory](../docs/lab/representation.html). Proposed next step: compare matched representations with consenting adults.

## Social Cymatics (June 12 and November 18, 2026 discussions)
Original prompt: could repeated sand contact reshape the vibrating plate that organizes it? Hammer analogy: sometimes a hammer strengthens steel, sometimes it knocks away debris. Distinguish structure, participants and forces as roles in a self-modifying system. Candidate mechanisms: reinforcement, erosion, resonance, sedimentation, hysteresis, reflexivity and threshold transitions. Examine intrasocial, extrasocial, parasocial and existential forces rather than collapsing them into one variable. See [research note](social-cymatics.md). Proposed example: matched fixed/adaptive rule simulations, vary repeated local interactions and shocks, measure structural memory.

## Human Solvency (January 8, 2026)
Proposed constraint stack: compute, power, water/cooling, materials/geography, institutional permission and human solvency. Human solvency is a proposed multidimensional capacity involving health, psychological stability, financial resilience and civic coherence, not an established measurement. Examples: compute per kilowatt-hour, closed-loop cooling, continuity under workforce/institutional stress. See [research note](human-solvency.md). Test incremental forecast value rather than assuming causality.

## Embedded Systems Intervention (August 17, 2026)
Original phrase: “sneak a system into a system.” Interpret as authorized, compatible intervention rather than covert access. Questions: where can I touch the rope, what changes if I alter one local rule, and how can I act intelligently in a system I cannot fully understand? Learning sequence: feedback → emergence → learning → networks → adaptation → constraints → probability → topology → intervention. Examples: ants and swarms, decentralized teams, knot invariants, a territory-assignment feedback routine. See [research note](embedded-systems-intervention.md). Compare local interventions against baseline and replacement.

## Thought Reserve (September 19, 2026)
Original speculative question: whether thinking consumes a finite resource related to consciousness ending, and how animal cognition could disconfirm it. Fictional prototype: 100 units, 20 per thought, finite vs renewable recovery; independent body signals. Further concept: capacity to turn an encounter into a new internal state may diminish even while memory or motor response persists. A relationship object loses adaptation; can participation disappear before presence? This is not physiological or mortality evidence. Original full prototype remains in its controlled source repository. See [clean illustrative demo](../docs/lab/reserve.html). Proposed next step: compare comprehension of replenishment rules; explore disconfirming evidence separately.

## Scope
Confidential ventures, manuscripts, unpublished proprietary research and personal records are intentionally excluded. Further recovered ideas should receive a dated entry, original prompt, concrete example, current artifact, evidence/limits, and an explicit next test.
