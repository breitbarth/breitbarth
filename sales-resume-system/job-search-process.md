# My job search process

By Aaron Breitbarth

I approach a job search the way I approach an outbound sales territory. Decide where to spend time, qualify the opportunity, show evidence that fits the problem, and give every conversation a next step. This is the workflow I use to connect my field sales, team leadership, and analytics experience to the work an employer needs done.

## 1. Define the roles worth pursuing

My target roles include sales leadership, sales operations, and sales analytics. I also consider direct sales roles where outbound prospecting and territory ownership matter. My search prioritizes remote startup and growth-stage roles and includes strong matches in the Greater Saint Louis area.

Write down the role families, geography or remote preference, compensation needs, and work you want to do. Decide which constraints are firm before comparing opportunities.

## 2. Build a shortlist from three sources

- **New postings.** Review a weekly shortlist with the employer, title, location, posting date, advertised compensation when available, application link, and a reason for the fit. Remove duplicate and expired listings.
- **People I know.** Reconnect with former colleagues, managers, and professional contacts when there is a specific company, role, or introduction to discuss.
- **Inbound recruiters.** Treat an inbound message as a lead. Confirm the employer or recruiting firm, the role scope, the location arrangement, and the compensation structure before investing in multiple calls or assessments.

Verify a posting on the employer's careers page when possible. Keep one record per opportunity even when it comes through several channels.

## 3. Qualify the opportunity

| Question | What I look for |
| --- | --- |
| What does the employer need? | A concrete sales, territory, team, or reporting problem |
| Can I show evidence? | One or two examples I can explain from my own work |
| Does the role fit? | Scope, location, compensation, and responsibilities that work for me |
| What can I do next? | Apply, request an introduction, ask a specific question, or pass |

For sales roles, clarify who owns prospecting, what the quota measures, how territories are assigned, and how base pay and variable pay work. For leadership roles, ask what team you would inherit and what success looks like in the first few months. Unknown information goes into the next conversation as a question.

## 4. Tailor the résumé and reach out

I keep a master record of my experience and pull the most relevant evidence into each application. For leadership, I lead with team attainment and field coaching. For direct sales, I lead with personally won customers and how I sourced them. For operations or analytics, I show how reporting and territory data changed a decision.

For example, when a role calls for territory management, I can explain how I worked with Survey and Construction to identify newly serviceable neighborhoods and redirect field coverage as those areas opened. If the role calls for coaching, I can describe how I worked alongside reps in live customer conversations. See the [Sales Résumé System](README.md) for the evidence worksheet and résumé template.

Use a short outreach note with three pieces: the role or problem you noticed, one relevant example, and one concrete ask. Save the résumé version you sent so you can prepare from the same document later.

## 5. Prepare for interviews as discovery conversations

For each interview, prepare two stories from the résumé and a few questions about how the employer works today. For a sales role-play, I ask about the current process, where it breaks down, a recent example, and the impact. I play back what I heard, connect it to one or two relevant capabilities, and ask for the appropriate next step.

After the conversation, log what you learned, what you promised, and when you expect to hear back. A follow-up can reference one specific point from the discussion and confirm the next step.

## 6. Review the pipeline each week

Use a simple tracker you can keep private:

| Employer / role | Source / posting link | Fit and open questions | Stage | Résumé version | Next action / date |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [Company / role] | [Posting, referral, or recruiter] | [Evidence of fit and what to verify] | [Shortlisted, applied, screening, interviewing, closed] | [Filename or date] | [One action and due date] |

Review which sources produced replies, which résumé versions led to interviews, and where conversations stalled. Pick one adjustment for the next week. If applications get replies but screenings stall, revisit role fit or how you explain your experience. If strong-fit applications get little response, test a clearer opening or a targeted introduction.

The same habits carry into sales work: set criteria for where to spend time, qualify before investing heavily, keep accurate records, and change your approach when the results call for it.
