Sales Leadership Field Notes

How I decide where to send a field sales team, what to coach, and how to check whether a change helped.

Aaron Breitbarth

Which addresses are ready for a sales visit?

A construction handoff can change where a representative should spend the afternoon. I check field observations and property records against the service release before assigning addresses.

Worked example. A new development has 80 addresses. Construction has confirmed service at 50. Those 50 enter the assignment queue. I track how long confirmation takes to reach a representative and use the eligible addresses when comparing results.

What does a sales number leave out?

Two towns can produce the same number of sales from very different numbers of conversations. I use a workbook organized by town, representative, attempt, and outcome. Contact rate tells me how often we reached someone. Sales per contact tells me what happened once we did.

That distinction changes the coaching conversation. On a field visit, I can observe a representative’s opening, agree on one change to try, and revisit it against comparable contacts.

How do people practices carry into the field?

  • Recruiting and onboarding. Define the skills the role requires. Use the same interview criteria, then revisit those skills at practice milestones.
  • Training. Give the team a short scenario, time to practice, peer feedback, and one thing to try on the next field visit.
  • Performance feedback. Record the observed gap, the desired outcome, the support offered, and the date to review progress together.
  • Recognition. Ask what people value. Use clear achievement criteria and check participation across the team.

Working with a multi-location customer

Start with the requirements at each location. Compare recurring costs on equivalent service terms, confirm the customer’s understanding, and agree on a follow-up. The examples on this page are generalized illustrations of the methods.
